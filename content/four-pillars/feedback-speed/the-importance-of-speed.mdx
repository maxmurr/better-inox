In the previous lesson, we argued that feedback is crucial for achieving any
goal. Now, we must stress the importance of speed in that cycle. It may sound
obvious that speed is a desirable attribute, but it's worth making it explicit.

Imagine that you're working on a new feature for your app - a whole new page
with forms to register attribute-based permissions for other users in the
platform. There are multiple steps to make it work:

- Ensuring the page has all the required form fields
- Ensuring the form fields are validated and masked
- Ensuring the data structure to hold the form data is appropriate
- Ensuring the communication with the back-end API

After evaluating all the requirements, you concluded that it might take one week
to finish that new page. Let us visualize that:

In that scenario, what happens if we only collect **feedback\*** at the end of
that week? We might have introduced a bug on day 2 and built the remaining
sub-features on top of it. The amount of rework would probably be considerable,
affecting most components and logic we've designed so far - and no one likes to
waste work and code. So, how do we improve that workflow? The answer is feedback
**speed.** We must collect feedback more quickly, so we can adjust the code,
requirements, and design accordingly. That means we'll work on shorter, mini
cycles of feedback:

<img
  src={'/images/four-pillars/iterative-changes.png'}
  alt={
    'Activity diagram: from Start, three dashed containers labelled iterartive and captioned Step 1, Step 2, and Step 3 run in sequence, each holding its own Change step with a decision diamond and a [No] loop back, before reaching the Goal end state.'

/>

_**Feedback** in this scenario is two-fold: determining whether we built the
right thing (the resulting page/component matches the requirement), and whether
we built the thing correctly (there are no bugs)_

Considering that each cycle above takes around a day, we can identify and
resolve bugs and misunderstandings of requirements **much faster**. Hence, we
acknowledge the importance of not only receiving feedback on our work but also
receiving it promptly.

But can this be improved even further? Collecting feedback after a day's work
could still make you lose an entire day if you did something wrong at the very
beginning. Let's find out in the next lessons.
