We can finally include the last metric, "Resistance to Refactoring," in our
comparison table to visualize the strengths and weaknesses of each test type.
Here's the final table:

```
|             | Maintainability | Feedback Speed | Protection      | Resistance     |
|-------------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Unit        | High            | High           | Low to Medium   | Low to Medium  |
| Integration | Medium          | Medium         | Medium  to High | Medium         |
| E2E         | Low             | Low            | High            | High           |
```

**Unit tests** ↳ They vary between low and medium resistance to refactoring.
Unit tests that cover internal functions, classes, or implementation details
often break. If we focus on units of behavior and public-facing functions,
however, we have much less brittle tests.

**Integration tests** ↳ Provide a good amount of resistance since they test the
behavior from the user's perspective, unaware of most implementation details.
However, we still have to know the shape of the input and output of external
dependencies, and if these change, the test will break.

**E2E tests** ↳ Provide the highest possible level of resistance. Pretty much
everything can change internally, as long as the described behavior remains the
same, the test will pass.

Now, what type of tests are the best? Which of these attributes should we focus
on?

The **two most important** attributes for **any** test are **Resistance to
Refactoring** and **Maintainability.** Whatever the layer you choose, the tests
should be trustworthy and as easy as possible to read, understand, and change.
That's why both circles above state "Max Out".

That being said, we are left to choose between **Protection** and **Speed.** If
we strive for higher protection, at the cost of speed, we should focus on **E2E
Tests.** On the other side of this spectrum, we have **unit tests**, which will
be the fastest possible — but at the cost of some protection.

Finally, in the very middle, we have **Integration Tests** - they strike the
best balance between protection and speed, which is precisely the reason we
recommend focusing on them.
